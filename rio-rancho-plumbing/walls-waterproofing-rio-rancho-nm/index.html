    Small crack patches, sure. A complete system takes knowledge of drainage, foundation behavior and how each product has to be applied, and mistakes are expensive because they hide behind finished walls for years. The footing joint is where most DIY jobs we see failed.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, heavy equipment, correct product thickness and an understanding of how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. Done wrong, it can trap water against the wall and cause more damage.

    Bubbling paint is a big one.     Sprinklers, nine times out of ten. Spray heads and drip lines aimed at the house soak the block every day, and poor grading or a downspout dumping at the corner adds more, so the masonry stays wet and leaves mineral deposits as it dries. Moving one sprinkler head can fix it.

    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before any sealer goes on, and for larger growth, roughly anything over ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. We remove smaller affected areas ourselves.
