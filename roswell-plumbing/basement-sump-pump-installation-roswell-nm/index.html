    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Roswell

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Cutting a new pit through the slab typically costs $1,500 to $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Visible rust and a stuck float are other red flags, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Age matters too.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. A secondary pump, often on a battery, waits in the same pit, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Each pump gets its own check valve.

    Quality cast iron pumps usually carry three to five years of factory coverage. Our labor has its own warranty on top of that, and we walk you through the exact terms for the model you pick before we install it.

    Swapping an identical plug-in pump is doable for a handy homeowner. Cutting a pit, wiring a new outlet and routing discharge are not, because a mistake there leads to a cracked slab, a shock hazard, or water pumped right back against the foundation in Roswell. Hire a licensed plumber for those.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Oversizing causes short cycling.

    Plan on 4 to 8 hours for a standard install. Digging a new pit and patching the slab can take the whole day, and tricky electrical access or a long discharge run through a finished wall adds time on top of that. You get a time estimate first.

    Grinding or rattling during a cycle is the first warning. Rust on the housing and constant on-off cycling are others, along with a pump that does not start during a heavy rain even though the pit is full to the float. Past ten years, replace it.
