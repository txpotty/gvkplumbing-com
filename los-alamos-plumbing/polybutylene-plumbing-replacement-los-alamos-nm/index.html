    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Los Alamos

    PEX replacements usually run $4,000 to $10,000, and copper often runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Attic access matters a lot.     Every poly replacement we do comes with a written warranty on materials and labor. PEX makers add their own. Both warranties come in writing with the photos and permit record, all in one place for later.

    Gray, blue or black plastic pipe marked PB2110 at the water heater or under sinks is the clearest sign. The build year matters. If your home was built between about 1978 and 1995, has this pipe and has had even one leak at a fitting, replacement is strongly recommended before the next one.

    Most full poly repipes take three to five days, including drywall patching. Big houses need more time. The schedule depends on the number of fixtures, attic access and whether you choose PEX or copper, and water is usually back on at the end of each work day.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A and Type L copper with Viega ProPress or soldered fittings. Your house and water decide which we suggest.

    For PEX, we use trusted brands like Uponor, Viega and Zurn. We use Uponor most often. For copper, we install Type L tubing from established mills rather than the thinner Type M found in many older homes, because the thicker wall holds up better over time.

    Both are good choices. PEX is cheaper. PEX is flexible, handles freezing and movement better and installs faster, while copper is rigid, tolerates heat and sunlight and has a long track record, so the right answer depends on budget, water and preference.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper can last longer. In water that does not attack it, Type L copper can last 50 years or more, and both are major upgrades over poly.
