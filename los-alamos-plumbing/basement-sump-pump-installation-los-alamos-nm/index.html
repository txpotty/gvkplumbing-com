    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Los Alamos

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Cutting a new pit through the slab typically costs $1,500 to $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. Most people add the backup.

    Every two to three months, and before and after the wet season. Dump a bucket of water into the pit slowly, then watch the float lift, the pump start, the water drop, and the pump shut off cleanly without humming or rattling. That is the whole test.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Oversizing causes short cycling.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. A sticking float or rust streaks on the housing also mean trouble, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Replace it before the next storm.

    Permit rules vary by town, including Los Alamos. Replacing a pump in the same pit rarely needs one, but a new pit, a new outlet or a new exit through the foundation wall often does, and we sort that out before any concrete gets cut. The paperwork is on us.

    Permits are typically required for water heater replacements, new gas lines, sewer work and most remodels that move fixtures. New pits, new circuits and new discharge routes are the parts most likely to need a plumbing permit. We check the local rules before the job starts, and when a permit applies we pull it and schedule the inspection ourselves. You do not deal with the office.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. Horsepower and lift decide which model goes in, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your Los Alamos basement actually takes on. We default to cast iron.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and a concrete patch takes most of a day, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.
