    Submersible Sump Pump Installation in Los Alamos, NM

    Submersible sump pumps are designed to sit directly in the sump pit, submerged in water, which makes them quieter and less intrusive.     Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Los Alamos

    A great pump is useless if the water it moves runs right back toward the foundation. We route rigid PVC discharge pipe out of the basement and extend it at least 10 feet from the house or into a pop-up emitter or dry well. A spring or swing check valve keeps the column of water from dropping back into the pit after every cycle, which saves wear on the motor. Even in milder parts of the state, clear winter nights can drop below freezing, so outdoor spigots and evaporative cooler lines need draining each fall. Where lines can freeze, we add a freeze-resistant outlet so ice cannot block the discharge.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Los Alamos

    Pedestal sump pumps are an excellent choice for narrow or shallow sump pits, as their motor sits above the water level. This design makes them easier to maintain and can often offer a longer lifespan because the motor stays out of the water. GVK Plumbing can properly install pedestal sump pumps in your Los Alamos home, with the right sizing and setup for effective water removal without crowding your pit.

    Sump Pump Pit Installation in Los Alamos, NM

    A properly built sump pit is fundamental to an effective sump pump system. If your basement lacks a suitable pit or needs an upgrade, GVK Plumbing provides professional sump pit installation.     Pedestal Sump Pumps in Los Alamos

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor above the pit, which makes them easier to service and a good fit for narrow pits in older homes. They typically last a long time because the motor never sits in water. The tradeoff is more noise and a less finished look, so we usually recommend them for utility rooms rather than living spaces. We install them with a rigid discharge, a quality check valve and a float rod set to your pit depth.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Los Alamos

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by finding the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor stays clean and level.
