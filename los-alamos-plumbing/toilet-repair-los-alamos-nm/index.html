    Leaking Toilet Repair in Los Alamos

    Water on the floor can come from four places: the wax ring, the tank-to-bowl gasket, the supply line or sweat running off a cold tank. Each one needs a different fix. We dry everything and watch where the water shows up first, which takes about ten minutes and saves pulling a toilet that did not need it. A cracked bowl gets a replacement quote, not a patch.

    Leaky Toilet Repair in Los Alamos

    Food coloring in the tank is the cheapest test we run. If color shows up in the bowl within fifteen minutes without a flush, water is leaking past the flapper or the flush valve seat, and the toilet is quietly refilling itself all day and night. A new flapper fixes most of these.

    Flush Mechanism Repair in Los Alamos, NM

    A weak flush usually means the tank is not filling to the fill line, the chain has too much slack, or the flapper is closing before the tank empties. We set the water level to the mark on the overflow tube and adjust the chain to about half an inch of slack. Handles break too. A loose or cracked handle is a five-minute swap.

    Toilet Flush Repair in Los Alamos, NM

    Two or three tries to get a full flush points to a flapper closing too early, a chain hooked in the wrong hole, scale in the rim jets, or a cracked overflow tube letting water bypass the bowl. Each one has a tell. We watch a full cycle with the lid off and replace the flush valve if the overflow tube is cracked, since it cannot be patched.

    Pressure-Assist and Dual-Flush Repair in Los Alamos

    Pressure-assist toilets use a sealed vessel inside the china that compresses air with incoming water pressure, and the cartridge inside that vessel eventually wears out. Hardware stores rarely stock those parts. We carry Flushmate cartridges and dual-flush seals for the common TOTO and American Standard models, and we check house pressure, since these units need steady line pressure to flush properly. Low pressure makes them sound weak.

    Running Toilet Repair in Los Alamos

    A tank that keeps hissing and refilling every few minutes is almost always losing water past the flapper or overfilling into the overflow tube. It adds up fast. We check the chain, the float height, the flapper and the seat before touching a part, then replace only what failed. Most running toilets are quiet again in about thirty minutes.

    Commercial Toilet Repair in Los Alamos

    Office and retail restrooms take more flushes in a day than a house sees in a month, so the parts wear faster and fail harder. Commercial bowls usually run on flushometer valves rather than tanks, and those need diaphragm kits and vacuum breakers from time to time. We carry Sloan and Zurn kits. Work can be scheduled before opening or after close.

    Clogged Toilet Clearing in Los Alamos, NM

    Most clogs sit in the trapway, the curved channel cast into the porcelain, and a closet auger with a rubber-sleeved head clears them without scratching the bowl. Plungers miss some. When the auger finds nothing and the bowl still fills, the blockage is further down the drain or in the main line. At that point we run a cable from the nearest cleanout or send a camera down to see it.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Repair in Los Alamos

    Flapper or fill valve repairs usually run $100 to $250 with parts and labor, while a wax ring reset lands closer to $200 to $400 depending on the flange. Clearing a clog with a closet auger is often $150 to $350. You get the exact number after we diagnose it. Nothing gets added to that number unless you sign off on extra work.

    Call us and we come back. Our workmanship guarantee covers the repair, so if the toilet starts running or leaking again from the same cause inside the warranty period, the return visit and the fix cost you nothing. Parts failures fall under the manufacturer warranty.

    Calcium and magnesium in hard water crust onto the flush valve seat, the fill valve ports and the rim jets under the bowl lip. That leads to slow refills and weak flushes over time. Descaling helps. Parts with fewer small ports also hold up better in hard water.

    Repair makes sense when the porcelain is sound and the toilet flushes well with new parts. A cracked bowl or tank usually points to replacement, a toilet that clogs every week no matter what we do, or an old 3.5 gallon model that wastes water on every flush. We give you both prices.

    A loose chain or a float set too high is a fair DIY fix, and so is swapping a flapper if you buy the right size for your flush valve. Take the old one to the store. Should it keep running after a new flapper, the seat or the fill valve is usually the problem, and that is a good time to call GVK Plumbing.

    Kohler, American Standard, TOTO, Gerber, Mansfield and Delta cover most of what we see, and we stock their common flappers and fill valves. Older brands like Eljer and Briggs are fine too. A discontinued model may need a part ordered, which usually takes a day or two.

    Swapping tank parts or resetting a toilet on a new wax seal generally does not need a permit. Moving the drain or adding a new toilet usually does, and the rules come from local code, so they vary from one town to the next even within New Mexico. We check before starting.

    Flapper and fill valve jobs take twenty to forty-five minutes. Pulling the toilet and resetting it on a new wax ring takes about an hour, and a flange repair adds another thirty to sixty minutes depending on what is under the floor. Deep clogs take longer.
