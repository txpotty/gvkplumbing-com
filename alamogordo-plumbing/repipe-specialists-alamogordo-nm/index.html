    PEX-A Whole-Home Repiping in Alamogordo

    PEX-A is flexible, resists scale and handles thermal expansion well, which suits hard water and long attic runs.     Slab Leak Repiping in Alamogordo

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Expansive clay soils in some areas swell and shrink with moisture, which can shift slab foundations and the pipes that run beneath them. Rather than jackhammer to reach one leak, we run new lines through the attic and walls and leave the old under-slab pipe out of service. No jackhammer needed. That stops the current leak and removes the chance of the next one under the same floor.

    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Alamogordo

    Old galvanized lines shed rust flakes that clog aerators, faucet cartridges and toilet fill valves. That is often the first sign. New PEX or copper stops the rust, restores pressure and ends leaks at the threads, which is where galvanized fails first. Old pipe stays in walls when pulling it would wreck finished surfaces.

    Galvanized Pipe Replacement in Alamogordo, NM

    Galvanized steel rusts from the inside, and over decades the inside diameter closes down to a fraction of what it was. Pressure drops slowly. Brown water after the house sits overnight, weak flow at upstairs showers and leaks at threaded joints are the usual signs that galvanized is near the end. New PEX or copper takes its place. Most homeowners notice the pressure difference the first time they turn on the shower.

    Drain Line Repiping in Alamogordo

    Drain pipe wears out too, especially old cast iron that cracks and scales up inside. Slow drains in several fixtures, sewer odors and repeated clogs in the same line are common signs of a failing drain system. A camera shows the condition. Failing sections are replaced with PVC or ABS sloped and vented to code, which drains faster and resists buildup much better than the pipe it replaces.

    Copper Repiping in Alamogordo, NM

    Some homeowners prefer copper for its long track record and rigidity. Our copper work uses thicker Type L pipe, joined with lead-free solder or ProPress fittings, rather than the Type M found in many older houses. Copper takes a bit longer. Copper repipes usually cost more and take a day longer than PEX, mostly because every turn needs a fitting.
