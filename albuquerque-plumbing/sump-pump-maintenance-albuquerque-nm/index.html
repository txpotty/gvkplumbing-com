    Float Switch Repair and Adjustment in Albuquerque, NM

    Float trouble causes more no-start calls than anything else. A tethered float can tangle on the pump body or catch on the side of the basin. Vertical floats hang up once minerals coat the rod. In tight pits where nothing has room to swing, we can switch you to an electronic sensor switch with no moving float at all.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Check-Ups in Albuquerque, NM

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the water, so the wear shows up in the shaft, the bearings and the float rod. The float rod gets checked for bends, and the stops get checked for slipping. Bearings get a listen. Rattling or a high whine at startup usually means the motor is near the end.

    Zoeller and Wayne Sump Pump Maintenance in Albuquerque

    Brand matters less than people think, but parts do not cross over cleanly between Zoeller, Wayne, Liberty and Little Giant. Our plumber reads the model number off the label and orders the matching float or seal kit. A submersible Zoeller and a pedestal Wayne need different checks. We know both.

    Emergency Sump Pump Service in Albuquerque

    A pump that runs nonstop, hums without moving water or will not start at all needs a plumber the same day, before the pit overflows.     Wayne Sump Pump Maintenance in Albuquerque, NM

    Some Wayne models draw water through a top suction intake, which helps them avoid pulling in the sludge that settles at the bottom of a pit. We check that intake and the float mechanism for buildup. Loose discharge fittings show up on every brand. Easy fix.

    Sump Pit Cleaning and Debris Removal in Albuquerque

    Silt and gravel wash into a pit through the drain tile every time it rains, and over a few years that layer can bury the intake screen. Our plumber drains the basin and digs the sediment out by hand. With the pit clean, the float swings without catching. The pump runs cooler too.

    Float Switch Checks in Albuquerque, NM

    The float switch tells the pump when to start and stop, and a float with too little travel between on and off makes the pump short cycle dozens of times an hour. Our plumber watches the float rise and drop through a complete cycle. Next we adjust the on and off levels so each run is longer and the starts are fewer.
