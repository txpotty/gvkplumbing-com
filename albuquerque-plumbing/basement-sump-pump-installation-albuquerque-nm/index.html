    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Albuquerque

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Cutting a new pit through the slab typically costs $1,500 to $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. The backup is a common add.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. Horsepower and lift decide which model goes in, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your Albuquerque basement actually takes on. We default to cast iron.

    A primary pump does the everyday work. A secondary pump, often on a battery, waits in the same pit, and it kicks on if the primary fails, loses power, or gets overwhelmed by more water than it can move during a heavy storm. Each pump gets its own check valve.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Once a year is enough for most pumps, ideally before the wet season. A tune-up covers the float, the pit, the check valve and the discharge, and we confirm the discharge outlet outside is open, since a clogged outlet makes the pump run hard against a blocked line. It takes under an hour.

    Plan on 4 to 8 hours for a standard install. Digging a new pit and patching the slab can take the whole day, and tricky electrical access or a long discharge run through a finished wall adds time on top of that. You get a time estimate first.

    Liberty Pumps, Zoeller and Wayne are our regulars. All three use cast iron housings and reliable switches, and all three have solid parts support through local supply houses, which matters more than a fancy feature list when something breaks. The pit size picks the model.

    A straight pump replacement usually does not need a permit. New pits, new discharge routes and new circuits may, so we check the local rules and pull permits when they apply.
