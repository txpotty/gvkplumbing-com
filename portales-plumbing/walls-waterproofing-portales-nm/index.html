    Wall Waterproofing Work We Handle in Portales, NM

    Below-Grade and Basement Wall Waterproofing in Portales

    Newer homes around Portales mostly use PEX or copper supply lines, while older properties may still have galvanized steel that restricts water flow. Any wall below grade sits in soil that gets saturated after heavy rain or snowmelt, and that water pushes against the wall with steady hydrostatic pressure. We inject cracks, coat the wall inside or out, and install drain tile where water pools along the footing. A sump goes in if groundwater sits high. Not every basement needs one.

    Drainage System Integration in Portales, NM

    Water has to go somewhere once a wall is sealed. Exterior footing drains, interior drain tile and surface French drains all do the same job at different depths. We match the drain to where the water comes from, which might be a high water table, a yard that slopes toward the house, or a gutter dumping at the corner. Downspouts get extended at least four feet out.

    Exterior Foundation Waterproofing in Portales

    Exterior waterproofing means digging down to the footing and coating the outside of the wall. It is the most dependable way to stop a basement leak, because the water never touches the block or concrete. We apply a rubberized asphalt or polymer membrane, add a dimple board to protect it, and set perforated drain pipe in gravel along the footing so water has somewhere to go. Caliche, a hard, cement-like soil layer, is common in New Mexico and can make digging for sewer and water line repairs slow work. We backfill after the drain is tested.

    Sump Pump Installation and Repair in Portales, NM

    A sump pump is the part of an interior system that actually moves the water. We set a lidded basin below slab level, install a cast iron pump sized to the water coming in, and add a battery backup or water-powered backup for the night the power goes out during a storm. Check valves matter. A missing check valve lets the discharge line drain back into the pit and the pump short-cycles. The discharge line gets routed far enough out that it drains away from the house.

    Crawl Space Vapor Barriers in Portales

    Bare dirt in a crawl space releases gallons of water vapor a day into the air under your floor. A 10 mil or heavier polyethylene barrier, lapped 12 inches at the seams and taped, cuts that off and keeps the joists and subfloor dry. We run it up the foundation walls and hold it in place with termination strip. Cheap fix, big difference.

    Crawl Space Encapsulation in Portales

    Encapsulation seals the whole crawl space, walls and floor, with a reinforced poly liner. We run 12 to 20 mil liner up the walls, tape every seam, seal around piers and pipe penetrations, and often add a dehumidifier to keep relative humidity below 60 percent. Dry air and intense sun are the main climate stress in New Mexico, drying out rubber seals and breaking down exposed PVC and hose bibs. Wood rot slows to a stop. Drier air under the floor also means less musty air upstairs.

    Shower Wall Waterproofing in Portales

    Tile and grout are not waterproof. Water passes through grout every time you shower, so the layer behind the tile, a membrane over cement board or a foam board system, is what actually keeps the studs dry. We tear out failed tile, install new backer, and apply Schluter Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban at the thickness the manufacturer calls for. We reinforce corners, niches and valve penetrations with fabric or preformed pieces.

    Basement Wall Sealing in Portales, NM

    Concrete block is porous, and its hollow cores fill with water like a sponge. Dry air and intense sun are the main climate stress in New Mexico, drying out rubber seals and breaking down exposed PVC and hose bibs. We drill weep holes at the bottom course to drain the cores, then coat the face with a masonry waterproofing product that lets vapor out but stops liquid water. The musty smell usually fades within weeks. The weep holes empty into interior drain tile when there is one.
