    Seasonal Plumbing Checks in Las Cruces, NM

    Dry air and intense sun are the main climate stress in New Mexico, drying out rubber seals and breaking down exposed PVC and hose bibs. A seasonal check looks at the parts of the system that weather affects most. We look at hose bibbs, exposed pipe in garages and crawlspaces, irrigation backflow devices and the water heater's relief valve and drain. Insulation gets checked too. Fixing a cracked hose bibb or adding insulation is a small job compared to a burst pipe in the wall behind it.

    Sewer Line Camera Inspection in Las Cruces, NM

    Older homes in Las Cruces often have cast iron or clay sewer pipe that cracks, scales up or lets roots in at the joints. The camera records every defect along with its distance from the cleanout. Then we locate it. With depth and position marked, repair pricing gets accurate, and choosing between a spot fix, a liner or replacement gets easier.

    Gas Connection Safety Check in Las Cruces, NM

    During a full inspection, we look at the visible gas piping and the connectors to each appliance. Old brass connectors, missing shutoffs or drip legs and rust all get noted, and we sweep joints with a gas detector. Any leak is reported on the spot. Most of our gas findings are in older homes, often at original appliance connectors.

    Water Heater Safety Inspection in Las Cruces

    A water heater stores a lot of hot water under pressure, so safety items matter. We test the T and P valve, confirm its discharge line runs to a safe place, look for an expansion tank where one is needed and check the drain pan. We check gas units for draft. A heater that backdrafts can spill combustion gases into the house, so we test the draft at the vent hood and note any problems right away.

    Hard Water Damage Assessment in Las Cruces

    Water softeners and filtration systems are popular around Las Cruces because they lengthen the life of fixtures and cut down on spotting. We look for crusty aerators, scaled showerheads, slow-filling toilets and sediment in the water heater, and we can run a simple hardness test on the spot. Then we explain your options. A softener, a filter or a scale-reducing system can extend heater and fixture life, and we tell you which one fits the hardness we measured.
