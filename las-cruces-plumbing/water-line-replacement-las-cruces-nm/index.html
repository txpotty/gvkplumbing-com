    Type L vs. Type K Copper in Las Cruces, NM

    The thickest wall belongs to Type K, the usual choice underground. Type L is thinner and mostly used inside the house. Some codes allow L for burial, but we stick with K for service lines because a thicker wall buys more years against corrosive soil and stray rocks. Same fittings, different pipe.

    PEX Water Line Installation in Las Cruces, NM

    PEX bends around corners, so a typical service run needs no fittings between the meter and the house. Pipes in exterior walls, attics and unheated garages around Las Cruces are the most likely to freeze when a cold front moves through. PEX can swell a little in a freeze and go back to shape, where copper splits. We still bury it below the frost depth the local code sets, and keep it covered from sunlight at the meter box, since UV light breaks it down. Short exposure only.

    Homes built before about 1950 sometimes have lead service lines. Scratch the pipe near the meter with a key. If it is soft, dull gray and turns shiny where scratched, it may be lead, and replacing it is worth doing soon. Many utilities now offer help with the street side.

    Start with the meter. Close the main shutoff at the house and watch the dial. A moving dial with the main closed means the leak is outside. If it stops, the leak is inside, perhaps a toilet flapper or a pipe under the floor, and that is a different repair.
