    Jet Pump Suction Line Repair in Las Cruces, NM

    Foot valves and suction lines cause most jet pump trouble, not the pump. Air sneaks in through a pinhole in the suction line, the pump loses prime and the seal burns up running dry. The suction line gets a pressure test. If the well is too deep for a jet pump, we will say so and quote a submersible.

    Well Pump Sizing in Las Cruces

    Sizing starts with the well log. The log, or a sounder down the casing, gives us static water level and well depth, and a draw-down test tells us roughly how many gallons per minute the well can actually give. Pump more than the well yields and it runs dry.

    Franklin Electric Well Pump Replacement in Las Cruces

    Franklin Electric builds a large share of the submersible motors sold in the US, including ones sitting under other brands' pump ends. Parts and control boxes are easy to find. We match the motor and the wire gauge to the depth of your well.

    What a Well Pump Job Costs in Las Cruces, NM

    A well pump job usually runs a few thousand dollars, and the pump itself is often less than half of that. Labor and hoist time, along with new wire and pipe, make up the rest. Every line is on the quote.

    Sandy Wells and Submersible Pumps in Las Cruces

    Sand kills submersibles early. A well that pumps sand will wear impellers down in a few years, so we check for grit in the old pump and talk about setting the new one higher off the bottom or adding a sand separator. Pump setting depth matters.

    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Las Cruces, NM

    No-water calls often end at the control box, not the pump. Three-wire pumps have a control box with a start capacitor and relay, and those parts fail far more often than the motor itself. We test them first. Swapping a capacitor costs a fraction of pulling a pump.

    Submersible Well Pump Replacement in Las Cruces

    A submersible pump hangs on drop pipe inside a 4-inch or 6-inch casing, often 100 to 400 feet down, so pulling one is a two-person job with a pump hoist. Sand in the impellers and lightning surges are the usual culprits. Before pulling, we test the windings with a megohmmeter. That tells us whether the problem is down the hole or up top.

    Pulling a Submersible Pump in Las Cruces

    Pulling a submersible means lifting the pump and every length of drop pipe out through the pitless adapter, sometimes hundreds of feet of it. On the way up, we look at the torque arrestor and the splices. Most jobs are done in a day.
