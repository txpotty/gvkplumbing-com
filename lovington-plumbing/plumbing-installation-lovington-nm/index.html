    Expansion Tank and Sewage Ejector Installs in Lovington, NM

    A closed water system has nowhere for heated water to expand, so pressure climbs every single time the heater cycles. An expansion tank absorbs that swing and keeps the relief valve from dripping. A PRV usually means you need one. Fixtures below the sewer line get an ejector system with a sealed basin, a check valve and a vent.

    Water Service Line Installation in Lovington, NM

    New water service lines run from the meter to the house, and the old one usually comes out in the same trench. Most homes sit on concrete slabs, so leaks under the floor often call for slab leak detection before any repair begins. The trenching equipment we bring depends on what we find on site. A new main shutoff goes in where the line enters. Depth and bedding protect the pipe. Pressure and flow get tested before the trench is closed.

    PEX Pipe Installation and Repiping in Lovington

    Pinhole leaks in copper keep coming back once the pipe walls start to thin. A PEX repipe ends that cycle. We run either a manifold with home runs to each fixture, which balances pressure, or a trunk-and-branch layout when the walls make home runs impractical. We cut small, clean access holes and patch them, or hand off to your drywall contractor if you prefer.

    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Lovington, NM

    Electric tankless heaters pull serious amperage, often 120 amps or more for a whole-house unit. We confirm your panel and service can handle the draw first, and a licensed electrician joins the job if they need an upgrade. Plumbing is the easy part. We set the flow rate and outlet temperature so the shower does not run cold when the dishwasher kicks on.

    Backflow Assembly Testing and Installation in Lovington

    Fire lines and irrigation systems account for most of the backflow assemblies we install. A certified tester checks each assembly on install day and the results go to the water provider. That starts the annual clock. Most jurisdictions want a retest every 12 months, and we can put you on a reminder list so it does not slip.

    Sump Pump and Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Lovington

    Basements and crawl spaces that take on water need a sump pump, and fixtures below the sewer line need an ejector. They are different pumps for different water, and one cannot stand in for the other. Sizing comes first. We match pump capacity to how fast the pit fills, put the pump on its own circuit and run the discharge with a check valve.

    Sump Pump Installation in Lovington, NM

    A sump pump that fails during a storm usually failed months earlier: a stuck float, a seized impeller, a dead check valve or a cord unplugged for the shop vac. The pit goes to proper depth, the primary pump is sized for the inflow, and a battery backup is added on request. Discharge placement matters most. The discharge gets carried well away from the foundation so the same water is not pumped twice.

    PEX Repiping in Lovington

    Copper repipes cost more and take longer, which is why most whole-house repipes today use PEX-A or PEX-B tubing with brass or poly fittings. You also hear less of it, since PEX softens water hammer. Freeze damage is less likely too. Even in milder parts of the state, clear winter nights can drop below freezing, so outdoor spigots and evaporative cooler lines should get draining each fall. We use fittings matched to the tubing brand.
