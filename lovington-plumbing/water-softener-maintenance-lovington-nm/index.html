    Control Valve Diagnostics and Repair in Lovington

    Leaks at the valve body, water running to drain around the clock, a motor that hums without moving or a display stuck on one cycle tend to mean the seal and spacer stack is worn out. On a Fleck 5600 the fix is a seal kit and a new piston, often done in under an hour without pulling the valve off the tank. Cheaper than a new head.

    Salt-Free Conditioner Service in Lovington

    Salt-free conditioners, usually template assisted crystallization units, do not remove hardness. A hardness test still reads high afterward. They turn the hardness into microscopic crystals that mostly pass through without sticking. Plan on new media every three to five years, and the sediment prefilter ahead of it needs changing every few months, which is the part most owners skip.

    Yearly Maintenance Plans in Lovington, NM

    Brine Tank Cleanout and Inspection in Lovington, NM

    Brine tanks collect a gray sludge of salt fines and insoluble grit, and it builds up faster with cheap rock salt. The tank gets emptied, scrubbed and rinsed. Then we look for cracks near the overflow elbow and the brine well and refill with fresh salt.

    Hardness Testing at the Tap in Lovington

    We test with a titration kit, counting drops until the sample changes color, because test strips are too rough for setting a valve. We take the raw sample from an unsoftened hose bib or with the softener on bypass. Softened water comes from the kitchen tap.

    Commercial Softener Service in Lovington

    Commercial units work harder. Restaurants and laundromats can burn through a softener's capacity in a single day, so twin-tank systems swap tanks, keeping one in service while the other regenerates. We set visits around your business hours so the water never goes hard during a rush.

    Salt Bridge Removal and Brine Tank Cleaning in Lovington, NM

    Humidity and cheap salt cause most salt bridges. A hard shell forms across the tank, and the salt underneath dissolves away, leaving a hollow. The softener keeps regenerating on schedule, but it draws plain water instead of brine, which leaves the resin without a recharge and hard water flows to every tap.

    Breaking Up Salt Bridges in Lovington

    To check for a bridge, we push a broom handle straight down through the salt. If it hits a hard layer and then falls through, there is a bridge. We break the crust, pull out the mushy salt underneath with a wet-dry vac, then check the brine well, safety float, brine line and grid plate before refilling to about half full.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Maintenance in Lovington

    Most service visits run $100 to $250. The low end covers both hardness tests and a manual regeneration. A heavily fouled tank or worn seals in the control valve pushes the price up, and you get the number before we start.

    Scale coming back is the giveaway. Dull laundry and dry skin after a shower point the same way. Higher salt use, a brine tank full of water, a grinding noise from the valve head or a drop in water pressure through the house can also mean the softener needs service.

    We service Fleck, Clack, Autotrol, Culligan, Kenmore, Morton, Whirlpool and GE units, plus most store brands sold at home centers over the last 20 years. The valve inside matters more than the label on the cabinet. Proprietary parts can take a few days.

    Water across New Mexico is generally hard and mineral-heavy, so scale accumulates quickly in water heaters, faucets and appliance valves. Calcium and magnesium are what leave scale in pipes and water heaters, and a softener that quits regenerating lets them right back in. One visit a year catches it before the scale does damage.

    In our experience, yes. A clean injector and fresh seals mean the valve is not straining through each cycle. Resin that gets cleaned when it fouls, instead of years later, often keeps working past ten years, and the valve body usually outlasts it.

    Evaporated salt pellets are the cleanest option and cause fewer bridges and less sludge than rock salt. Solar crystals land somewhere in the middle. Potassium chloride works if you want to cut sodium, but it softens less per pound, so the salt dose usually has to go up by a quarter or more.

    Once a year works for most homes. Every six months makes sense for very hard water or a heavily used older unit, especially with a tankless heater downstream. Between visits, look in the brine tank once a month and watch for spots on glassware.
