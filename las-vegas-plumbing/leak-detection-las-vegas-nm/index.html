    Our Leak Detection Equipment in Las Vegas

    Our trucks carry ground microphones and correlators, thermal cameras, line tracers, moisture meters and tracer gas kits. Each tool has a job. Acoustic gear hears pressurized water escaping, thermal cameras show temperature differences from hot water lines and wet drywall, while tracer gas catches silent leaks on PEX and PVC.

    Pool Leak Detection in Las Vegas

    A pool losing more than a quarter inch a day beyond evaporation likely has a leak somewhere. We start with a bucket test. Then we pressure test the suction and return lines, use an underwater hydrophone to listen, and dye test around the skimmer, lights and main drain so we can point to the exact fitting or crack.

    Gas Leak Detection in Las Vegas

    A faint sulfur smell near the range or furnace needs a professional check. Get out first if it is strong. Our licensed plumbers use a combustible gas detector and soap solution on fittings, appliance connectors and the meter riser, then pressure test the line to find a leak too small to smell, and we repair it to the fuel gas code before turning service back on.

    Leaks in Walls and Ceilings in Las Vegas, NM

    A brown ring on the ceiling under a bathroom usually points to a shower valve, a drain joint or a toilet wax ring. Our job is figuring out which. Thermal cameras show the cool wet area, a moisture meter confirms it and a borescope through a small hole lets us see the actual pipe without opening the whole ceiling.

    Irrigation Leak Detection in Las Vegas, NM

    Sprinkler laterals crack and zone valves stick open, and either one can waste thousands of gallons before the bill shows it. Check the yard first. Our tech tests the irrigation system separately from the house, runs each zone and traces buried lines, since sprinkler leaks are the first thing most water departments suggest checking.

    Slab Leaks From Ground Movement in Las Vegas

    Pipe set in concrete cannot flex when the ground moves, so it cracks at elbows and where it rises through the slab. Most homes sit on concrete slabs, so leaks under the floor often call for slab leak detection before a repair begins. We isolate the line first. Then acoustic listening and thermal imaging find the leak so nobody has to break the floor open to search, and you get a marked spot plus repair options before the saw comes out.

    Underground Water Line Leak Detection in Las Vegas, NM

    The buried line from your meter to the house can leak for months without surfacing, especially in old galvanized pipe. Green grass in a dry spell is a clue. We trace the line with an electronic locator, then use a ground microphone to hear the leak, and on plastic pipe we inject tracer gas and sniff for it at the surface.

    Preventive Leak Inspections in Las Vegas, NM

    Checking for leaks once a year catches the small stuff. We test static pressure, read the meter with everything off and look at supply lines, water heater fittings and shutoff valves. Older homes with galvanized pipe or original water heater connectors get the most out of this, because those parts often fail together. It is a short visit.
