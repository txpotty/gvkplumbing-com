    Frequently Asked Questions About Water Line Installation in Las Vegas

    Residential water lines usually run $1,800 to $5,500 depending on length, soil and material. Rock, boring or copper can raise the total to $6,000 to $10,000 or more.

    PEX and copper are the usual picks, with HDPE on long runs. PEX flexes with ground movement and resists corrosion, and copper is rigid, durable and resists bacterial growth, which is why plenty of owners still prefer it for buried service. Many rural homes rely on private wells, and some groundwater carries naturally occurring arsenic or high mineral content that calls for testing and treatment. The deciding factors are usually cost and water chemistry.

    This is a job for a licensed plumber. It involves excavation, sizing, connections and code rules, plus coordination with the water utility at the meter, and a poor install can lead to leaks, water damage and contamination of your drinking water. The risk is not worth it.

    Low pressure everywhere in the house points at the main, not one fixture. Other clues include rusty water and a soggy stretch of lawn, especially along the path between the meter and the house, and a bill that jumps without a reason usually confirms it. Old lines fail this way.

    Expansive clay soils in some areas swell and shrink with moisture, which can shift slab foundations and the pipes that run beneath them. Expansive clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry, which pushes and pulls on buried pipe. That cycle can crack rigid pipe or separate a joint over time, so we favor flexible PEX or HDPE, sand bedding and a little slack in the line where the ground moves most in Las Vegas. Flexible pipe forgives movement.

    Almost always. A new or replaced main typically needs a plumbing permit from the building department, and the inspection confirms the work meets code before it gets buried. We file the paperwork for you.

    In most communities the homeowner owns the line from the meter to the house, and the utility owns the meter and the street side. Your water provider confirms the split before we start.

    PEX is flexible, cheaper, and has fewer fittings, so there are fewer places for a leak to start. Copper costs more but has decades of history behind it, and it handles heat and pressure well, so the right choice depends on your budget and the water chemistry in Las Vegas. Both pass code.
