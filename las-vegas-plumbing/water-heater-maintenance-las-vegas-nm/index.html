    Tankless Descaling in Las Vegas, NM

    Scale is the tankless unit's main enemy. Water across New Mexico is generally hard and mineral-heavy, so scale builds up quickly in water heaters, faucets and appliance valves. We close the isolation valves, connect a small pump and circulate descaling solution through the heat exchanger for the time the manufacturer recommends, then flush it with clean water. We clean the inlet filter. We also check the burner and venting.

    Tune-Up Versus Replacement in Las Vegas, NM

    A tune-up costs a fraction of a new heater. Installed tanks often run well over a thousand dollars, and regular flushing and anode replacement help a tank reach the high end of its rated lifespan, while tankless units can last many years with proper descaling. Sometimes replacement makes sense. You see both prices side by side.

    Descaling Tankless Units in Las Vegas

    Error codes often mean scale. Scale in the exchanger cuts flow and trips the unit's safety shutoff. We descale through the service valves. Yearly descaling is standard advice for hard water.

    Anode Rod Checks in Las Vegas

    The anode rod rusts so the tank does not. It is a long metal rod screwed into the top of the tank, and once it has corroded down to bare wire, the water starts eating the steel tank lining instead. We pull it and look. A new anode costs far less than a new tank.

    Tank Flush and Inspection in Las Vegas

    Sediment forms from minerals in the water. On a standard tank visit we flush that sediment out, test the temperature and pressure relief valve, check the anode for corrosion and inspect every fitting for leaks, which protects one of the more expensive appliances in the house. Efficiency comes back. Recovery speeds up.
