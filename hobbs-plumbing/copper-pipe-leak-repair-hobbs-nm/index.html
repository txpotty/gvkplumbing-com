    Frequently Asked Questions About Copper Pipe Leak Repair in Hobbs

    The cost of copper pipe leak repair in Hobbs can vary a lot depending on the leak's location, severity and accessibility. Minor repairs, like a simple solder patch, might range from $200 to $500.     Type K, L and M copper pipes differ mainly in their wall thickness and thus their pressure ratings. Type K has the thickest walls and is used for underground service and main water lines. Type L is common for interior water supply and radiant heating. Type M has the thinnest walls and is typically used for less demanding applications. We are skilled in repairing all types.

    Copper pipe leaks are often caused by corrosion, especially from hard or acidic water. Many rural homes rely on private wells, and some groundwater carries naturally occurring arsenic or high mineral content that requires testing and treatment. Other causes include excessive water pressure, improper installation, shifting foundations putting stress on joints and freezing temperatures that can cause pipes to burst. Pinhole leaks are a typical symptom of internal corrosion.

    Most straightforward copper pipe leak repairs, such as fixing an accessible pinhole leak or a single joint, can be completed within one to four hours. More complex issues, like leaks under a slab or extensive re-piping, may take a full day or sometimes multiple days, depending on the scope of work.

    To prevent future copper pipe leaks, consider installing a whole-house water softener if you have hard water, as mineral buildup can lead to corrosion. Maintaining appropriate water pressure, insulating pipes for cold weather and scheduling periodic plumbing inspections can also significantly extend the life of your copper pipes.

    Most accessible repairs take one to two hours. Leaks inside walls or ceilings can take two to four hours, and slab leak repairs may take a whole day.

    Common signs of a copper pipe leak include unexplained wet spots on walls, ceilings or floors, a sudden drop in water pressure, the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use, unusually high water bills or visible blue-green stains on pipes or nearby surfaces that point to corrosion.
