    Main Water Line Repair in South Valley

    Your main line is the pipe from the meter to the house, usually 3/4 inch or 1 inch, buried anywhere from a foot to several feet down depending on frost depth. A leak here shows up as a soggy strip of lawn and a meter dial that never rests. We start by closing the house shutoff and watching the dial. If it still moves, the leak is buried. Then a ground microphone narrows the spot, so the crew opens one small pit rather than trenching the whole run.

    Repair vs. Replace Your Water Line in South Valley, NM

    One leak on a newer copper or PEX line is a spot repair. Two or more leaks on galvanized is a replacement. Age matters too, and a line past about 40 years is usually near the end no matter the material. Spot repairs often run a few hundred dollars while a full replacement costs more and resets the clock for decades. You see the cut-out section yourself.

    Emergency Water Line Break Repair in South Valley, NM

    A full break can flood the yard and leave the house dry at the same time. Shutting the water off comes first. The crew pumps the hole, finds the break and decides whether a coupling will do or whether a section needs replacing, and if the repair will run past a few hours we set a temporary bypass to the house. Most breaks are fixed the same day. We photograph the damage for your insurance or HOA.

    Water Pressure Restoration in South Valley

    Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line or the regulator, not the faucets. First step is a pressure gauge on the outside spigot. Then we check the pressure reducing valve, the main shutoff and the curb valve for anything half closed, and look at the pipe itself. Old galvanized can be choked to half its size with rust. The fix might be a new valve or a new line.

    Pipe Bursting and Pulling in South Valley

    Pipe bursting means splitting the old line in place while the new one follows behind it. Straight runs are ideal. A cone-shaped head breaks the old pipe outward, and a length of HDPE trails behind it in the same path. You end up with a fresh line and two small pits, and the lawn, sidewalk and driveway in between stay where they are, which is the whole reason people pick this method over a trench.

    Galvanized Water Line Replacement in South Valley, NM

    Galvanized service lines were common in houses built before the 1970s, and a lot of them are still in the ground. Water softeners and filtration systems are popular around South Valley because they lengthen the life of fixtures and cut down on spotting. Rust and scale close the pipe from the inside. Rusty water at the first tap in the morning is a classic sign, along with pressure that drops when two fixtures run at once. Replacing the full run with copper or PEX clears all of it up. Usually a one-day job.
