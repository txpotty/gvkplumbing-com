    Clay and Orangeburg Pipe Replacement in South Valley, NM

    Clay pipe came in short sections with a joint every two or three feet. That is a lot of joints for roots to find. Orangeburg is worse, since the tar-paper wall softens and deforms under the weight of the soil, and both are good candidates for bursting because they break apart easily. Fast pulls.

    Trenchless Pipe Bursting in South Valley

    Brittle pipe bursts cleanly. A hydraulic rig at the receiving pit pulls the bursting head through the old line, splitting it into the soil, while a continuous length of fused HDPE follows behind so the new pipe has no joints at all between the pits. Roots cannot get into a joint that does not exist.

    Commercial Sewer Line Replacement in South Valley

    Sewer Line Material Upgrades in South Valley

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all failed for different reasons. PVC avoids all three of those problems. Schedule 40 or SDR 35 PVC is what most codes call for on a lateral, choosing the wall thickness based on how deep it sits and what drives over it. Glued joints stay sealed.

    Roots find sewer lines by following moisture. A hairline crack is enough. Once inside, they grow into a mat that catches paper and grease, and a root cutter clears them for a season at most, because the opening is still there. New PVC with glued joints gives them no way in.
