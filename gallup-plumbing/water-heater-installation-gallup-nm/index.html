    A traditional tank water heater typically lasts between 8 and 12 years with proper maintenance. Tankless water heaters often have a longer lifespan, ranging from 15 to 20 years, thanks to their durable components and on-demand operation.

    The typical lifespan of a traditional tank water heater is about 10 to 15 years, while tankless water heaters can last 20 years or more with proper maintenance. Factors like local water quality, maintenance frequency and usage habits can all influence the actual lifespan of your unit. Regular flushing can help extend its life.

    The decision to repair or replace often depends on the age of your unit, the cost of the repair and its energy efficiency. If your water heater is over 10 years old, often breaks down, or the repair cost is more than 50% of a new unit, replacement is often the more economical choice. Newer models also offer much better energy efficiency.

    Most households of two to four people do well with a 40 or 50 gallon tank. Larger families may need a 75 gallon tank or a tankless unit sized by flow rate.

    Rusty water, leaks around the base, rumbling noises and running out of hot water quickly are common signs. If your heater is over 10 years old, replacement is often wise.
