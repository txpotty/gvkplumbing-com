    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Gallup

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. A brand new pit through the slab falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and a battery backup adds roughly $600 to $1,500. The backup is a common add.

    Changing out a pump in an existing pit takes about two to three hours. Plan on most of a day for a new pit with a slab cut and patch, since the concrete needs a little time to firm up before the pump goes into service.

    Look for a pump that runs nonstop, short cycles, or does not start when the pit fills. Corrosion on the housing and a float that hangs up are warning signs, and if your basement flooded even though you have a pump, the pump or its switch was not up to the job. Age matters too.

    Our installs carry a written warranty on both parts and labor. You will see the exact length in your service agreement, and the pump manufacturer adds its own coverage, which on a quality cast iron pump usually runs three to five years from the date of purchase. We register it for you.

    A 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump handles most residential basements. What decides it is how fast the pit fills and how high the water rises, so we time the refill with a stopwatch and measure from the pit floor to the discharge exit before picking a model. Oversizing causes short cycling.

    Watch for constant running, rapid cycling or grinding noises. Rust stains and a float that sticks are warning signs too, as is water sitting above the switch level without the pump starting. Replace it before the next storm.

    Zoeller, Wayne, Little Giant and Liberty Pumps cover most of our installs. We pick the model by horsepower and lift, then by features like a vertical float or a built-in alarm, so the pump fits your pit, your budget and the amount of water your Gallup basement actually takes on. Cast iron is our default.
