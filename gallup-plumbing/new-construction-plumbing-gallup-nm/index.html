    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Gallup

    Price depends mostly on fixture count and finish level. Two baths in a basic house costs far less than four baths with tankless and recirculation in a custom build. We price from your plans.     Rough-in on a typical house takes one to two weeks of crew time, spread over the build. Underground runs a day or two before the pour. Top-out happens after framing and takes three to five days, then trim waits until cabinets and counters are in, which can be months later on a custom home. Trim is one or two days.

    PEX-A is our default for water supply in New Mexico homes. It tolerates hot water and avoids the pinhole leaks copper can develop in some water. We run drains in Schedule 40 PVC. Joints under the slab are kept few, and every turn uses a long sweep so a cable can pass.

    We install Moen, Kohler, Delta and American Standard, plus most other major brands a builder or designer specifies. We can set fixtures you buy yourself. We ask that they arrive on site before trim day so we can open the boxes and check for missing parts, because a missing cartridge or wrong finish can push trim back a week.

    Slow drains in a brand-new house are the first sign. Gurgling fixtures or sewer odor point to venting or slope problems. A camera inspection through the cleanout will show a belly, an offset joint or debris left in the line, and it is worth doing before the builder's warranty period ends.     We do, mostly on custom homes. We work from your architect's plans and suggest changes that shorten runs or improve flow.     Most jurisdictions inspect plumbing three times. First comes underground, with drains holding a water or air test before the pour.
