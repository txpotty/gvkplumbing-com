    Frequently Asked Questions About Slab Leak Detection in Artesia

    Slab leak detection in Artesia typically costs $150 to $450. Repairs range from about $800 for a simple reroute to $3,000 or more for direct access under finished floors. We provide a written estimate after locating the leak.

    Slab leaks can cause significant damage including foundation cracks, soil erosion beneath the slab, mold and mildew growth in wall cavities or under flooring, damage to flooring materials and increased humidity levels. Over time, it can lead to structural instability and major, costly repairs.

    Common signs of a slab leak include unusually high water bills, warm spots on your floor, the sound of running water when no fixtures are in use, visible cracks in the foundation, damp or wet spots on carpets or flooring and a sudden drop in water pressure. Musty odors can also indicate moisture buildup.

    Caliche, a hard, cement-like soil layer, is common in New Mexico and can make digging for sewer and water line repairs slow work. Hard water can also corrode copper pipes from the inside. Pipes can rub against concrete or gravel as they expand with hot water, and over time these factors cause pinholes and cracks.

    Direct repair works well for a single leak on otherwise healthy pipe in an accessible spot. Rerouting is often better under tile or hardwood and avoids future leaks on that line. We help you weigh both.

    Reroutes and repipes often require a plumbing permit, while small direct repairs may not. Requirements vary by city and county. We handle permits and inspections when needed.

    While the detection process itself typically doesn't require a permit, any subsequent repair work involving the excavation of the slab or significant plumbing alterations will likely require a plumbing permit from the local building department. GVK Plumbing handles all necessary permits for repair work.

    Common pipe materials prone to slab leaks include older copper pipes susceptible to corrosion from hard water or soil acidity, and sometimes PEX or CPVC if they were improperly installed or exposed to extreme temperatures. Galvanized steel pipes, though less common under slabs today, can also corrode and leak.
