    Why a Sewer Camera Inspection Belongs on Every New Mexico Home Inspection

    By Andrew Blake · August 13, 2026 · 5 min read

    What a Standard Home Inspection Misses

    A general home inspector runs the faucets and flushes the toilets, and that is about where the drain check ends. The sewer lateral, the pipe from the house to the public main or septic tank, never gets looked at. It is also the most expensive pipe on the property to replace. Sellers rarely know its condition.

    Our crews at GVK Plumbing inspect lines for buyers across New Mexico, and the camera work rarely takes more than an hour. It costs a small fraction of a new sewer line. Vacant houses are the riskiest, because a line can sit broken for months with nobody flushing to reveal it.

    Most homes sit on concrete slabs, so leaks under the floor often call for slab leak detection before a repair begins.

    Older laterals were often clay tile or cast iron, and years of shifting soil and growing roots break both of them down. Clay joints separate and let roots in. Cast iron rusts along the bottom until the waste is running over dirt instead of pipe. You will not see either problem from inside until something backs up.

    What the Camera Shows

    The camera goes in through a cleanout or a pulled toilet and travels the full length of the line out to the main. A locator on the camera head lets us mark the exact spot and depth of any problem on the ground above. You get a recording. Here is what we look for.

    • Root intrusion at joints
    • Bellies, or sags, that hold standing water
    • Cracks, breaks and offset joints
    • Corroded or collapsed cast iron
    • Orangeburg pipe that has started to flatten
    • Grease buildup or debris from a past backup

    Septic systems are very common in rural New Mexico, and many homes outside town limits pair them with a private well.

    On a septic property, the camera also shows the line from the house to the tank. That run gets missed often. Roots or a belly near the tank inlet show up often, and pumping the tank does nothing for them. Request the last pumping date and any drain field paperwork from the seller. A camera does not test the drain field itself, so a septic inspection is a separate step.

    Inspection Cost Versus Repair Cost

    Sewer line repairs range from a single spot repair to a full lateral replacement under the driveway. A spot repair might be a day of work, while a full replacement under concrete can take a week and a lot of restoration. Once you own the house, that bill is yours. Before closing, it is a negotiating point.

    Buyers use the video in a few ways. Some ask for a price reduction or a repair credit at closing. Others ask the seller to have the repair done by a licensed plumber with a permit before closing, then rerun the camera to confirm it. The video is evidence.

    Scheduling an Inspection

    Book the camera inspection during your inspection period, not after. Most purchase contracts only give you a short window, often somewhere around 7 to 10 days, to raise any repair issues. You can reach GVK Plumbing at (505) 581-4975. Have the address and the year the house was built ready when you call. We can usually get a camera out within a couple of days, and we send the video to you and your agent the same day.
