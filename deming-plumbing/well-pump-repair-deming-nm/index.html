    Low Water Pressure Repair in Deming

    Weak pressure on a well is not always the pump. Common causes include a plugged sediment filter, a low switch setting or a yard line leak. Sometimes the pump is fine and the well is simply producing less water than the house is asking for during peak use. We test each one.

    Well Pressure Tank Repair in Deming

    Bladder tanks hold a cushion of air that lets the pump rest between cycles. A failed bladder lets the tank waterlog, and the pump begins to short cycle. We inspect the bladder, the switch and the tank connections. Proper sizing adds years to a pump.

    A dead pump leaves the house without toilets, showers or drinking water. GVK Plumbing answers emergency well pump calls throughout Deming and nearby areas and puts them ahead of routine work.     Deep Well Pump Repair in Deming

    Deep wells get submersible pumps. They push water up instead of pulling it, and they run quiet for years, but motors, impellers and wire splices still fail eventually. We pull the pump with the right equipment, test it and replace only what failed. Heat-shrink splice kits and a torque arrestor protect the new work in your Deming, NM well.

    Constant Pressure System Repair in Deming, NM

    Variable speed drives like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by changing motor speed. When they fault, the drive shows a code that guides diagnosis. The drive, sensor and wiring get tested, and the failed part gets repaired or replaced before we verify the setpoint. These need a smaller tank.

    Jet Pump Repair in Deming, NM

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Lost prime, seal leaks and impeller wear account for most jet pump calls. Our crew in Deming restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for leaks that let air in.     Control Box and Surge Repair in Deming, NM

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Summer monsoon storms can bring sudden heavy rain to New Mexico, causing flash flooding that overwhelms drainage and backs up into low areas. Surges from lightning are the usual cause of control box failures. After testing the capacitor, relay and overload, we replace the bad part and suggest a surge arrestor if the box lacks one. An arrestor costs little.
