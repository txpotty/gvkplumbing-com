    Control Box and Surge Repair in North Valley, NM

    Three-wire submersible pumps rely on a control box with a start capacitor and a relay. Water conservation is a priority across the state, so low-flow fixtures and efficient irrigation upgrades are common requests. Lightning and power surges damage those parts more than anything else. We test capacitors, relays and overload protection, replace what failed and recommend a surge arrestor at the box if one is missing. An arrestor costs little.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in North Valley

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Pitted contacts, a clogged sensing tube and ant nests are the usual reasons one fails. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank, typically 40 and 60 psi. This is among the least expensive well repairs.

    Submersible Well Pump Repair in North Valley

    Submersible pumps hang inside the well casing on drop pipe, often 100 feet or more below the wellhead. The usual signs are no water at all, weak pressure or a hum with no flow. We test the motor from the surface first. When a pull is needed, a hoist brings the pipe up safely so we can replace the motor, the impellers or the check valve.

    Franklin Electric Well Pump Service in North Valley, NM

    Franklin Electric motors and control boxes are in a lot of local wells. Our technicians know them well, from the QD control boxes to the SubDrive constant pressure units. We carry the common Franklin parts. A motor that tests bad on a megohm meter usually gets replaced rather than rewound, since a new motor costs less than shipping and rebuilding the old one.

    Jet Pump System Repair in North Valley, NM

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Air leaks on the suction side cause most failures. GVK Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. Nonstop running without pressure usually means lost prime or an air leak.
