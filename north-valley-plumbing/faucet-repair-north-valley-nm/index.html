    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Repair in North Valley

    The cost of faucet repair in North Valley can range from $120 to $350, depending on the type of faucet, the specific parts needed, and the complexity of the repair. A simple washer replacement will be on the lower end, while a cartridge replacement in a high-end faucet might be more. We provide a written estimate before any work begins.

    Our plumbers are skilled at repairing all major faucet brands commonly found in New Mexico homes, including Delta, Moen, Kohler, American Standard, Pfister, and many others. We keep a stock of universal and common brand-specific parts to speed up repairs.

    For a standard faucet repair, where only internal components are replaced and the fixture itself remains, no plumbing permits are typically required in North Valley. However, if the repair involves altering the existing plumbing lines or installing a new type of fixture that requires new connections, a permit might be necessary. We will let you know if any permits are needed for your specific situation.

    Simple cartridge swaps can be a DIY job if you identify the correct part. Stuck cartridges, corroded valve bodies and leaks under the sink are better handled by a plumber.

    Faucet repair involves fixing the existing fixture by replacing specific worn or broken internal components like washers, O-rings, or cartridges. Faucet replacement means removing the old faucet entirely and installing a brand new one. We recommend replacement when a faucet is severely damaged, very old, or if you simply want an upgrade.

    Most repairs take 30 to 60 minutes. If a special part must be ordered, we may need a second short visit.

    If the faucet body is solid and parts are available, a repair is generally worth it. If it is corroded, cracked or discontinued, a new faucet is often the better choice.

    While a minor drip isn't typically an immediate emergency, it's a problem that should be addressed quickly to prevent water waste and potential damage. However, a rapidly gushing leak or a faucet that won't shut off completely is an emergency, requiring immediate professional attention to avoid serious water damage.
