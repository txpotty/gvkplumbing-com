    What a Tank Service Visit Covers in Clovis, NM

    Electric Element Check in Clovis

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. We test each one with a meter. If the hot water quits halfway through a shower, the lower element is the first suspect. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Expansion Tank Service in Clovis, NM

    A thermal expansion tank is the small steel tank on the cold line above the heater, and it gives heated water somewhere to go when a check valve or pressure reducer closes the system. We test the air side with a tire gauge. Precharge should match your house water pressure. A waterlogged expansion tank sounds solid when you knock on it, and it lets pressure spike every time the burner fires.

    Standard Tank Tune-Up in Clovis

    A standard tune-up starts with the fuel off and the cold supply closed, then a full drain through the bottom valve until the water runs clear. Figure on half an hour or a bit more for the drain. We check the heating elements or burner, read the thermostat setting and look at every fitting on top of the tank for green crust or drips. Any weeping at the dielectric nipples goes on the report.

    Burner and Pilot Service in Clovis, NM

    On a gas tank, a dirty burner throws a lazy yellow flame and leaves soot on the flue baffle. We pull the burner assembly and brush it clean. We also check the thermocouple or flame sensor, since a weak one lets the pilot go out every few days and leaves you resetting it on your knees. The goal is a steady blue flame.

    Sediment Flushing in Clovis, NM

    Sediment is mostly calcium carbonate and sand, and it settles in a layer that can reach several inches on a neglected 50-gallon tank. The heat has to push through it to reach the water. Our method is short bursts of line pressure that churn up the bottom. The drain hose runs to a floor drain or outside, never onto a lawn where hot water can burn the grass.

    Emergency Tank Troubleshooting in Clovis

    No hot water at all is usually a pilot, a gas valve, a tripped high-limit or a dead element. Most of those are quick fixes. With a leak, we first check whether water comes from a top fitting or from under the jacket. Leaks from under the jacket mean the tank itself has failed.

    Electric Thermostat Service in Clovis

    Each element on an electric tank has its own thermostat, and the upper one carries a reset button for the high-limit switch. When the reset trips, something caused it. We find out why it tripped, which usually comes down to a shorted element or a thermostat stuck closed, before we press it back in. Power stays off at the breaker the whole time.

    T&P Valve Replacement in Clovis

    Replacing a relief valve is a short job, but it has to be done right. The new valve must match the tank's BTU or kilowatt rating. We drain the tank below the valve opening, thread in the new one with pipe dope rated for potable water, and reconnect a discharge pipe in copper, CPVC or another approved material. Never plug or cap that pipe.

    Yearly, for most homes. Hard water or heavy use calls for every six months. Manufacturers like Rheem and Bradford White list annual flushing in their owner manuals, and a dated service record helps if you ever file a sediment-related warranty claim.

    Popping or rumbling, hot water that runs out sooner than it used to, rusty or cloudy water from the hot side, a sulfur smell, or moisture around the base. Any one is reason to call. A drip from the relief valve discharge pipe also counts, since it can mean high pressure or a failing valve.
