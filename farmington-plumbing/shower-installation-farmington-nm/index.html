    How We Install Showers in Farmington, NM

    Shower Stall Installation in Farmington

    A one-piece stall needs a level, fully supported base. We set fiberglass and acrylic bases in a bed of mortar or setting compound so the floor of the unit does not flex underfoot, since flex is what cracks a base within a couple of years. Then the drain goes in. We check the flow at the head once the trim is on.

    Shower Pan Installation in Farmington

    Shower pans fail quietly. Pinholes and loose drain clamps let water into the subfloor slowly, which is why tiled pans sit flooded for a full day before tile. We level prefab pans carefully. Both kinds get slope toward the drain.

    Prefab Shower Unit Installation in Farmington, NM

    Prefab units are the fastest route to a new shower. We set the base on mortar, connect the drain, install the valve and trim and make sure the walls are fastened so nothing flexes when someone leans on them. Good for rentals and guest baths. Most installs finish in a day.

    Tub-to-Shower Conversion in Farmington, NM

    Tub-to-shower conversions are one of our most common jobs. The tub comes out, the drain shifts to the pan location and the valve moves up to about 48 inches. Slab homes need concrete work. The new shower is safer to step into.

    Barrier-Free Walk-In Showers in Farmington, NM

    Barrier-free entries need careful drain placement. We work with your tile setter on the waterproofing membrane and the slope, since water has to reach the drain before it can roll out the open side of the shower. High elevation means large daily temperature swings, and that constant expansion and contraction stresses pipe joints and fittings. Pan material follows from that.

    Shower Valve and Fixture Installation in Farmington, NM

    Pressure-balancing valves are the baseline for any new shower. Thermostatic valves hold a set temperature and add volume control, which works well with rain heads and handhelds. Both prevent scalding. The outlet count usually decides it.

    Tiled Walk-In Shower Installation in Farmington

    Frameless glass walk-ins are popular, and the plumbing behind them has to be dead on. We install the valve, the head, an optional handheld and the drain, then set the mortar bed or foam pan to slope toward the drain at a quarter inch per foot. Testing follows. The valve gets a pressure test and the pan a 24-hour flood test before tile.
