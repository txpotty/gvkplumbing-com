    Cured-in-Place Pipe Lining in Santa Fe

    The old pipe gets cleaned first. Then the liner goes in. A flexible liner saturated with epoxy resin is inflated against the pipe wall and cured with hot water, steam or UV light, and once it hardens, it forms a smooth, jointless pipe inside the old one. Lining works well for cracks, root intrusion and separated joints.

    CIPP Lining for Clay and Cast Iron in Santa Fe

    Older Santa Fe homes often have clay or cast iron sewer pipe. They wear out. Roots get into clay joints and cast iron rusts from inside, and a liner seals over both with jointless pipe that should outlast the original. Only one or two access points are needed.

    Sewer Lateral Lining in Santa Fe

    Laterals are the most common candidates for trenchless repair. Clay and Orangeburg fail often. Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg pipe crack, sag or fill with roots over time, so we jet the line clean, inspect it by camera and choose lining or bursting based on what we see. The new line restores full flow and stands up to roots.

    Pipe Bursting in Santa Fe, NM

    Bursting is used when the old pipe is too damaged to line or needs a larger diameter. It needs two pits. A bursting head is pulled through, fracturing the old pipe outward while a new HDPE pipe follows behind, and that HDPE is fused into one continuous length with no joints at all.

    Trenchless Water Line Replacement in Santa Fe, NM

    Main water lines can also be replaced without a trench. Bursting or boring works. New HDPE or PEX goes in along the old path by bursting, or along a new one by directional boring, leaving lawns and drives alone. The line is pressure tested before service is restored.

    Pipe Bursting Services in Santa Fe, NM

    Bursting replaces a pipe that is too far gone to line. The old pipe gets split. A winch pulls a bursting head through the line, cracking the old pipe outward into the soil while new HDPE follows right behind it, so the replacement pipe ends up in the same path. HDPE resists corrosion and leaks in Santa Fe, NM soil.
